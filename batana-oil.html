How to use it
  1. Warm it. Scoop a little and rub it between your palms until it melts. It is solid in the jar — that is what unrefined looks like.
  2. Work it in. Scalp first, then down the lengths. More on the ends than the roots.
  3. Leave it. Twenty to sixty minutes, or overnight under a cap or scarf.
  4. Rinse and shampoo. It is heavy, so most people need two washes. Once or twice a week is plenty.

What it is

A mask, not a styling oil.

Batana oil is pressed from the nut of the American palm, and has been used on hair and scalp for generations in La Moskitia, on the Caribbean coast of Honduras. Unrefined, it is not a clear liquid — it is a thick, amber-brown butter that softens in your hands.

It is an occlusive: it sits on the hair and holds moisture in rather than sinking away. That is why it is used as a mask and rinsed out, and why it suits coarse, coiled, dry and heat-damaged hair, and ends that break faster than they grow.

What it is not: a leave-in, a styling oil, or a medicine. It conditions and protects the hair you already have.

Questions

Everything else

Will this regrow my hair?

No — and we would rather say so than sell you on it. Batana oil conditions, softens and protects the hair you have, and less breakage can look like growth over months. It is not a treatment for hair loss. If you are losing hair, see a doctor.

Ingredients

Elaeis oleifera (batana) nut oil.

That is the whole list. Unrefined means nothing was stripped out and nothing was added — no fragrance, no preservative, no carrier oil bulking it out. It sets solid below about 24°C and melts in your hands, which is the oil behaving normally rather than a fault.
