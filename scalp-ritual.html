The active

The copper peptide

  • GHK-Cu — three amino acids bound to a copper ion. The body makes it, and makes less of it with age. Isolated from human plasma in 1973.
  • The copper is why it's blue. GHK binds it tightly, and that complex is the form used in cosmetics.
  • It degrades in air — hence nine sealed ampoules, not one bottle going flat.

How to use it

Twice a week — Monday and Thursday, say. Five steps.

  1. Start clean and dry. Nothing else on the scalp.
  2. Wipe the head with isopropyl alcohol. 70% on a cotton pad, then let it air-dry. Every session, before and after — water doesn't disinfect it.
  3. Screw the head onto one ampoule. The bottle is now the handle.
  4. Roll slowly along a parting. Two or three drops per section. Let the weight of the head do it — pressing harder just hurts.
  5. Massage it in and leave it. No rinsing. Go to bed.

Wipe the head with alcohol again when you're done, let it air-dry, and keep it covered. One head, one person — never share it.

Nine sessions is about a month. Give it the full course before you judge it — the ninth lands around day 29, inside the 30-day guarantee.

Questions

Everything else

Will this regrow my hair?

Here is what it does. At 0.2 mm it carries a copper peptide serum into the surface of the scalp as you roll, rather than leaving it on the hair above to rinse off. It conditions the scalp, and what it is sold for is hair that looks fuller where you part it. That is a cosmetic job, and it is the one we are selling.

What it is not is a hair-growth treatment, and we can't promise you one. The clinical work on microneedling for hair used depths of 0.5 mm and above, and nearly all of it paired the needling with minoxidil — the approved drug doing the work. This kit is 0.2 mm and there is no drug in it. If you are losing hair, see a doctor: there are treatments approved for exactly that, and this is not one of them.

On the copper peptide specifically: GHK-Cu has a real research record, but the weight of it is in skin — wound repair and collagen. The hair work is early, mostly cell culture rather than people, and nobody should sell it to you as settled. We are not going to.

Does it hurt?

At 0.2 mm most people describe it as prickly rather than painful — closer to a stiff brush than a needle. Roll slowly, never press, and stop if your scalp is sore. A little redness that settles within the hour is ordinary; anything more than that means stop.

Who should not use it

Patch test on a small area first. Not for broken skin, sunburn, active irritation, an inflamed or infected scalp, or over moles. One head, one person — never share it, and replace it if the surface is ever visibly damaged. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or already on a prescription scalp treatment, ask your doctor before you start.
